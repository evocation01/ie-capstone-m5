Problem Definition
Mitigating the Bullwhip Effect in Modern Retail

Research Objectives
Accuracy Benchmarking
Compare Classical (ARIMA/HW) vs. ML (LightGBM) vs. Deep Learning (LSTM) performance.
System Scalability
Implement a "Dual-Track" pipeline using Polars to process 59M rows of Walmart data.
Financial Impact
Quantify "Value of Information" through Total Logistics Cost (Holding + Stockout) simulation.

Data Characteristics: M5 Challenge
Scale: 30,490 SKUs across 10 Walmart stores.
Volume: 1,913 days of history; ~58 Million melted rows.
Complexity: High seasonality and hierarchical dependencies.
Intermittency: Massive zero-inflation (sparse sales patterns).

Demand Volatility: High product variety (30,490 SKUs) leading to "zero-inflated" or intermittent demand patterns.
The Bullwhip Effect: Small retail demand fluctuations causing amplified variances upstream, leading to waste.
Financial Imbalance: The constant trade-off between excessive holding costs and the risk of costly stockouts.
Modeling Gap: Traditional statistical methods struggle with exogenous variables like price changes and SNAP holidays.

Model Mechanic: LightGBM
Leaf-wise Decision Trees
•
Leaf-wise Growth: Splitting based on max loss reduction; superior for Walmart's scale.
•
Regression Objective: Optimized using Mean Squared Error (MSE) for the target sales column.
•
EFB: Exclusive Feature Bundling reduces the high-dimensional SKU categorical space.
•
Histogram Binning: Discretizes continuous prices to accelerate training.

Model Mechanic: LSTM
Neural Sequence Modeling
•
Gating Logic: Uses Forget, Input, and Output gates to maintain long-term memory.
•
Global Approach: A single network learns shared patterns across all 30k products.
•
Embeddings: Learns high-dimensional representations of Store and Item IDs.

Classical
ARIMA, Holt-Winters, and Smoothing. Robust for stable cycles but limited by univariate history.
Machine Learning
LightGBM and XGBoost. Superior at mapping prices, SNAP events, and calendar covariates.
Deep Learning
PyTorch LSTM and Transformers. High capacity for global patterns across millions of rows.

Forecast Accuracy Results (CA_1 Pilot)
Average RMSE across 3,049 items in Store CA_1. Lower is better.

The "Sparsity Penalty"
Why LSTM Underperformed
On zero-sales days, LSTM predicts a "safe mean" (e.g., 0.3 units) instead of a true zero.
Operational Friction: Fractional forecasts trigger unnecessary reorders, exploding holding costs in sparse retail environments.
LightGBM's tree-splits handled zero-inflation significantly better.

Managerial Recommendations
Algorithm Choice: Use LightGBM/GBDT for sparse retail SKUs; LSTM requires significant "zero-handling" tuning to be viable.
Tooling ROI: Polars is essential for IE systems involving big data (50M+ rows) to avoid hardware bottlenecks.
Decision Support: IE focus should shift from minimizing RMSE to minimizing Total Logistics Cost to align with business KPIs.
